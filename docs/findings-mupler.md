## Reading these numbers

Written by hand. Everything above this line is generated by `bun run report --dataset=mupler`.

MuPLeR-fi is the public half of this study: 10 000 EU legal passages, 200
queries with one gold passage each, EUPL-1.2, reproducible by anyone. The private corpus
corpus in `FINDINGS.md` is employer data and cannot ship.

### The question was worth thirty points

The first run of this corpus used the relevance judgment written for the private corpus
corpus, which requires the passage to come from the document the question names.
MuPLeR passages carry a bare numeric id, so that condition can never be verified
and a Noul that cannot verify its condition answers no. Same model, same
shortlists, same metrics:

| relevance judgment | `hybrid+jev-noul` R@1 | `hybrid+jev-batched` R@1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| written for a different corpus | 66.5% | 77.0% |
| written for this one | 95.5% | 97.0% |

Without reranking at all, `hybrid` scores 73.0%. The mis-specified question made
reranking *actively harmful*, and nothing about the model, the corpus or the
retrieval changed between those two rows — only the wording of one criterion and
the removal of two state fields that carried no information here.

That is the largest single effect measured anywhere in this repository, and it
is larger than every model difference combined. The mis-specified run is kept at
`results/mupler/latest-mis-specified-question.json`.

### Batched Jev reaches the retrieval ceiling

`hybrid+jev-batched` scores 97.0% R@1 against a shortlist ceiling of 97.0%. It
puts the gold passage first on **every query where the first stage retrieved it
at all**. There is no headroom left above it at this shortlist depth — a better
reranker cannot exist here, only a better first stage.

It also beats the dedicated cross-encoder (Voyage `rerank-2.5`, 95.5%) at the
same latency, and both chat models by four points and more.

### It knows when it is right

The operational result, at document level, top-1 correct:

| threshold | `jev-batched` answered / precision | `voyage` | `llm-4.1-mini` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | 96% / 99% | 98% / 97% | 90% / 94% |
| 0.8 | 78% / 99% | 56% / 99% | 87% / 95% |
| 0.9 | **54% / 100%** | 11% / 100% | 86% / 95% |

Gated at 0.9, batched Jev answers more than half the queries with no top-1
errors at all. Voyage reaches the same precision over 11% of queries; neither
chat model exceeds 95% at any threshold. For a system that must hand uncertain
cases to a person, coverage at a fixed precision is the number that decides the
staffing, and this is where the gap is widest.

### Cost

`jev-batched` is \$0.60 per thousand queries against Voyage's \$0.51,
`gpt-5.6-luna`'s \$2.76 and `gpt-4.1-mini`'s \$5.09. Batching is again what
makes it cheap and fast: 400 requests instead of 6000, 334 ms instead of 892 ms
at p50, and slightly *better* accuracy than the per-call form.

### What this benchmark does not show

- **The queries are generated, not asked.** MuPLeR's questions were written from
  their gold passage, so semantic paraphrase matching is exactly the skill being
  rewarded. Real user questions are shorter, vaguer and worse spelled.
- **The task is easy at depth 10.** Every reranker scores 97.0% R@10, the
  ceiling. All of the separation is at R@1.
- **One gold passage per query.** Nothing here measures behaviour when several
  passages are partially relevant.

The private corpus corpus in `FINDINGS.md` has none of those properties — real questions,
hundreds of near-identical documents, section-level ground truth — and there the
ordering is different. Both are in this repository for that reason.
